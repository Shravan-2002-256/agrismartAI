# Agricultural Knowledge Base - Crop Diseases and Treatments

## Common Crop Diseases

  Early Blight (Tomato)
Early blight is a fungal disease affecting tomato plants, characterized by dark brown spots with concentric rings on lower leaves. It typically appears during warm, humid weather and can spread rapidly if not controlled.

 Symptoms: 
- Dark spots with target-like concentric rings on older leaves
- Yellow halos around spots
- Premature leaf drop
- Fruit spots near the stem

 Treatment: 
- Remove and destroy infected leaves immediately
- Apply copper-based fungicide (2-3 grams per liter)
- Improve air circulation between plants
- Water at soil level, avoid wetting foliage
- Apply mulch to prevent soil splash

 Prevention: 
- Maintain 18-24 inch spacing between plants
- Rotate crops every 2-3 years
- Use disease-resistant varieties
- Apply preventive fungicide before rainy season

  Late Blight (Tomato & Potato)
Late blight is a devastating disease caused by water mold, capable of destroying entire crops within days during favorable conditions.

 Symptoms: 
- Water-soaked irregular lesions on leaves
- White fuzzy growth on leaf undersides
- Brown-black lesions on stems
- Firm brown rot on fruits

 Treatment: 
- Remove infected plants immediately
- Apply systemic fungicide (Metalaxyl or Mancozeb)
- Increase spray frequency during humid weather
- Ensure good drainage

 Prevention: 
- Plant certified disease-free seeds
- Avoid overhead irrigation
- Apply preventive copper spray weekly during monsoon
- Monitor weather conditions closely

  Powdery Mildew
Powdery mildew appears as white powdery patches on leaves and stems, affecting many crops including tomatoes, cucumbers, and grapes.

 Symptoms: 
- White powdery coating on leaves
- Distorted new growth
- Yellowing and premature leaf drop
- Reduced fruit quality

 Treatment: 
- Spray with sulfur-based fungicide
- Use baking soda solution (1 tablespoon per gallon)
- Apply neem oil weekly
- Improve air circulation

 Prevention: 
- Avoid excessive nitrogen fertilization
- Space plants adequately
- Remove infected parts promptly
- Water early morning

  Bacterial Spot
Bacterial spot affects tomatoes and peppers, causing significant yield losses during warm, rainy weather.

 Symptoms: 
- Small dark spots with yellow halos
- Leaf yellowing and defoliation
- Fruit lesions with raised margins
- Severe defoliation in advanced stages

 Treatment: 
- Apply copper-based bactericide
- Remove infected plant parts
- Avoid working with wet plants
- Use drip irrigation instead of overhead

 Prevention: 
- Use certified disease-free seeds
- Practice crop rotation
- Maintain proper plant spacing
- Sanitize tools regularly

  Septoria Leaf Spot
Septoria leaf spot is a common fungal disease of tomatoes, appearing as numerous small spots on leaves.

 Symptoms: 
- Small circular spots with gray centers
- Dark brown margins around spots
- Black dots (spores) in spot centers
- Lower leaves affected first

 Treatment: 
- Remove infected lower leaves
- Apply chlorothalonil or copper fungicide
- Mulch around plants to prevent soil splash
- Increase plant spacing

 Prevention: 
- Stake and prune plants properly
- Remove plant debris after harvest
- Rotate with non-host crops
- Avoid overhead watering

## General Disease Prevention Practices

  Cultural Practices:
1. Crop Rotation: Change crop families every 2-3 years
2. Sanitation: Remove plant debris and weeds regularly
3. Proper Spacing: Allow 18-24 inches between plants
4. Water Management: Water at soil level early morning
5. Mulching: Apply organic mulch to prevent soil splash

  Monitoring and Early Detection:
- Inspect plants every 3-4 days
- Check leaf undersides for early signs
- Monitor weather conditions
- Keep records of disease occurrence
- Act immediately upon detection

  Soil Health:
- Add organic compost monthly (100-150 grams per plant)
- Maintain soil pH 6.0-6.8 for most vegetables
- Ensure proper drainage
- Use balanced fertilizers
- Avoid over-fertilization with nitrogen

  Biological Control:
- Encourage beneficial insects
- Use Trichoderma for soil-borne diseases
- Apply Bacillus subtilis for bacterial diseases
- Maintain biodiversity in the garden

  Chemical Control Guidelines:
- Read and follow label instructions
- Rotate fungicide classes to prevent resistance
- Apply during cooler hours (early morning/evening)
- Use proper protective equipment
- Observe pre-harvest intervals
- Keep records of applications
