# Crop Disease Knowledge Base

## Tomato Diseases

  Early Blight
 Pathogen:  Alternaria solani
 Symptoms:  Dark brown spots with concentric rings on older leaves, yellowing around spots, defoliation in severe cases
 Treatment:  
- Apply copper-based fungicides every 7-10 days
- Remove and destroy infected leaves
- Ensure proper spacing for air circulation
 Prevention:  Rotate crops every 3 years, avoid overhead irrigation, mulch to prevent soil splash

  Late Blight
 Pathogen:  Phytophthora infestans
 Symptoms:  Water-soaked gray-green spots on leaves, white mold on leaf undersides in humid conditions, rapid plant death
 Treatment: 
- Apply systemic fungicides (chlorothalonil, mancozeb)
- Remove infected plants immediately
- Improve drainage
 Prevention:  Use resistant varieties, avoid overhead watering, monitor weather for blight-favorable conditions

  Septoria Leaf Spot
 Pathogen:  Septoria lycopersici
 Symptoms:  Small circular spots with gray centers and dark borders, lower leaves affected first
 Treatment: 
- Apply fungicides containing chlorothalonil or copper
- Remove infected leaves weekly
 Prevention:  Stake plants for airflow, water at soil level, apply mulch

  Bacterial Spot
 Pathogen:  Xanthomonas spp.
 Symptoms:  Small dark spots on leaves and fruit, yellow halos around spots, leaf drop
 Treatment: 
- Apply copper-based bactericides
- Remove severely infected plants
- Avoid working with wet plants
 Prevention:  Use certified disease-free seeds, rotate with non-host crops, disinfect tools

  Tomato Mosaic Virus
 Pathogen:  Tomato mosaic virus (ToMV)
 Symptoms:  Mottled light and dark green on leaves, leaf distortion, stunted growth, reduced fruit
 Treatment: 
- No chemical treatment available
- Remove and destroy infected plants immediately
- Disinfect tools with 10% bleach solution
 Prevention:  Use resistant varieties, control aphid vectors, wash hands before handling plants

  Healthy Tomato
 Characteristics:  Vibrant green leaves, no spots or discoloration, uniform growth, strong stems
 Maintenance: 
- Water deeply 1-2 times per week
- Fertilize every 2 weeks with balanced NPK (10-10-10)
- Prune suckers for better airflow
- Monitor regularly for early disease signs

## Potato Diseases

  Early Blight
 Pathogen:  Alternaria solani
 Symptoms:  Brown spots with concentric rings on older leaves, stem lesions, tuber infections
 Treatment: 
- Apply mancozeb or chlorothalonil fungicides
- Hill soil around plants to protect tubers
 Prevention:  Rotate crops, destroy crop residue, use certified seed potatoes

  Late Blight
 Pathogen:  Phytophthora infestans
 Symptoms:  Water-soaked lesions on leaves, white fungal growth, rapid collapse, tuber rot
 Treatment: 
- Apply systemic fungicides immediately
- Harvest early if severe
- Destroy infected tubers
 Prevention:  Plant resistant varieties, monitor forecasts, ensure good drainage

  Healthy Potato
 Characteristics:  Lush green foliage, no lesions or spots, vigorous growth
 Maintenance: 
- Water consistently (1-2 inches per week)
- Hill soil when plants reach 6 inches
- Fertilize at planting and mid-season
- Harvest when foliage dies back

## Pepper (Bell Pepper) Diseases

  Bacterial Spot
 Pathogen:  Xanthomonas campestris
 Symptoms:  Small water-soaked spots on leaves, brown scabby lesions on fruit, defoliation
 Treatment: 
- Apply copper-based bactericides
- Remove infected plant parts
- Improve air circulation
 Prevention:  Use disease-free transplants, rotate crops, avoid overhead irrigation

  Healthy Pepper
 Characteristics:  Dark green glossy leaves, no spots, firm fruit development
 Maintenance: 
- Water at base of plants
- Fertilize every 2-3 weeks
- Provide support for heavy fruit
- Harvest regularly to encourage production

## General Disease Prevention Strategies

  Cultural Practices
1.  Crop Rotation:  3-4 year rotation with non-related crops
2.  Sanitation:  Remove crop debris, disinfect tools, clean hands
3.  Water Management:  Water at soil level, morning watering preferred, avoid leaf wetness
4.  Spacing:  Follow recommended plant spacing for airflow
5.  Mulching:  Organic mulch prevents soil splash and maintains moisture

  Monitoring
- Inspect plants weekly for early disease signs
- Check undersides of leaves
- Monitor weather conditions (high humidity = higher disease risk)
- Keep records of disease occurrences

  Soil Health
- Test soil pH (6.0-6.8 for most vegetables)
- Add organic matter annually
- Ensure good drainage
- Avoid soil compaction

  Resistant Varieties
- Tomato: 'Iron Lady', 'Mountain Magic', 'Defiant'
- Potato: 'Kennebec', 'Elba', 'Defender'
- Pepper: 'Enterprise', 'Guardian', 'Aristotle'

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

  Chemical Control (Last Resort)
- Use least-toxic options first (copper, sulfur)
- Rotate fungicide classes to prevent resistance
- Follow label instructions precisely
- Observe pre-harvest intervals

  Biological Control
- Encourage beneficial insects (ladybugs, lacewings)
- Use Bacillus subtilis for fungal diseases
- Apply compost tea for plant immunity

  Physical Control
- Remove infected plant parts immediately
- Use row covers to exclude pests
- Install drip irrigation
- Stake/trellis for airflow

## Emergency Response Protocol

  High Severity (Immediate Action Required)
1. Isolate infected plants
2. Apply appropriate treatment within 24 hours
3. Remove severely affected plants
4. Monitor adjacent plants daily
5. Consider harvesting early if necessary

  Moderate Severity (Action Within 3 Days)
1. Apply fungicide/bactericide treatment
2. Remove infected leaves
3. Adjust watering practices
4. Increase monitoring frequency

  Low Severity (Routine Management)
1. Continue regular monitoring
2. Improve cultural practices
3. Apply preventive treatments
4. Document for future reference
