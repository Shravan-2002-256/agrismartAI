# Sustainable Farming Best Practices

## Soil Health Management

  Soil Testing
 Frequency:  Annual testing before crop season
 Parameters to Test: 
- pH Level (optimal: 6.0-7.0)
- NPK (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium)
- Organic Carbon Content
- Micronutrients (Zinc, Boron, Iron)

 Interpretation: 
-  pH < 5.5:  Acidic soil, add lime
-  pH > 8.0:  Alkaline soil, add sulfur
-  Low Organic Carbon:  Add compost/FYM

  Organic Matter Enhancement
1.  Farmyard Manure (FYM):  10-15 tons per hectare
2.  Green Manure:  Grow dhaincha, sunhemp before main crop
3.  Compost:  5-7 tons per hectare
4.  Vermicompost:  2-3 tons per hectare (premium quality)

  Cover Cropping
 Benefits:  Prevents erosion, adds nitrogen, suppresses weeds
 Recommended Crops: 
- Legumes: Cowpea, horsegram (nitrogen fixation)
- Grasses: Oats, rye (biomass generation)
- Mustard: Biofumigation against soilborne diseases

## Water Conservation Techniques

  Drip Irrigation
 Advantages: 
- 40-60% water savings vs. flood irrigation
- Reduced weed growth
- Fertigation capability (apply fertilizer through water)
- Better for disease management (dry leaves)

 Crops Suitable:  Tomato, pepper, cucumber, watermelon
 ROI:  Payback in 2-3 years through water savings + higher yields

  Sprinkler Irrigation
 Advantages: 
- 30-40% water savings
- Uniform water distribution
- Suitable for field crops

 Crops Suitable:  Wheat, potato, vegetables, fodder
 Types:  Rain guns, mini-sprinklers, center pivot

  Rainwater Harvesting
1.  Farm Ponds:  30' x 30' x 10' can store 27,000 liters
2.  Percolation Tanks:  Recharge groundwater
3.  Roof Water Collection:  Store from farm structures
4.  Mulching:  Reduces evaporation by 50%

  Mulching Materials
-  Plastic Mulch:  Black (weed control), silver (reflects heat)
-  Organic Mulch:  Straw, leaves, grass clippings
-  Living Mulch:  Low-growing cover crops

## Integrated Nutrient Management

  Balanced Fertilization (per hectare)

 # Tomato
- Nitrogen: 150-200 kg
- Phosphorus: 60-80 kg
- Potassium: 100-150 kg
- Application: 50% basal + 25% at flowering + 25% at fruiting

 # Potato
- Nitrogen: 120-150 kg
- Phosphorus: 80-100 kg
- Potassium: 100-120 kg
- Application: 100% P & K + 50% N basal, remaining N at hilling

 # Wheat
- Nitrogen: 120-150 kg
- Phosphorus: 60-80 kg
- Potassium: 40-60 kg
- Application: 50% N + 100% P & K basal, 25% N at CRI, 25% N at anthesis

  Organic Alternatives
-  Neem Cake:  Slow-release nitrogen + pest repellent
-  Bone Meal:  Phosphorus source
-  Wood Ash:  Potassium source
-  Azotobacter:  Biofertilizer for nitrogen fixation
-  PSB (Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria):  Enhances P availability

## Crop Rotation Strategies

  Principles
1.  Alternate Deep/Shallow Rooted:  Rice → Wheat → Chickpea
2.  Include Legumes:  Fix atmospheric nitrogen (40-60 kg N/ha)
3.  Break Pest Cycles:  Different crop families
4.  Soil Health:  Alternate heavy feeders with light feeders

  Recommended Rotations

 # 3-Year Rotation
Year 1: Rice (Kharif) → Wheat (Rabi)
Year 2: Maize (Kharif) → Chickpea (Rabi)
Year 3: Cotton (Kharif) → Mustard (Rabi)

 # Vegetable Rotation
Year 1: Tomato → Cabbage
Year 2: Beans → Carrot
Year 3: Cucumber → Onion

 # Break Crops for Disease Management
- After tomato/potato (solanaceous): Grow cereals/legumes
- After cabbage/cauliflower (brassicas): Grow alliums/cereals
- After cucurbits: Grow leafy greens/legumes

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)

  Cultural Control
1.  Crop Rotation:  Breaks pest life cycles
2.  Sanitation:  Remove crop residues
3.  Trap Crops:  Marigold attracts aphids away from main crop
4.  Companion Planting:  Basil repels aphids from tomatoes
5.  Timing:  Adjust planting dates to avoid peak pest periods

  Mechanical Control
-  Hand Picking:  For large insects (caterpillars)
-  Yellow Sticky Traps:  Whiteflies, aphids, thrips
-  Light Traps:  Nocturnal moths
-  Bird Perches:  Encourage natural predators
-  Netting:  Physical barrier against insects/birds

  Biological Control
 Predatory Insects: 
- Ladybugs: Eat aphids (1 ladybug = 5,000 aphids in lifetime)
- Lacewings: Consume aphids, mites, whiteflies
- Trichogramma wasps: Parasitize caterpillar eggs

 Microbial Pesticides: 
- *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt): Caterpillar control
- *Beauveria bassiana*: Fungus kills whiteflies, aphids
- *Metarhizium anisopliae*: Controls beetles, borers

  Chemical Control (Last Resort)
 Biopesticides (Preferred): 
- Neem oil: Repels 200+ insects
- Karanj oil: Similar to neem
- Garlic extract: Aphid deterrent
- Chili-garlic spray: Homemade pest repellent

 Synthetic Pesticides: 
- Use only when economic threshold reached
- Rotate chemical groups to prevent resistance
- Follow safety precautions (PPE, pre-harvest intervals)
- Avoid spraying during flowering (protect pollinators)

## Climate-Smart Agriculture

  Heat Stress Management
1.  Mulching:  Reduces soil temperature by 3-5°C
2.  Shade Nets:  30-50% shade for sensitive crops
3.  Anti-Transpirants:  Kaolin clay spray reduces water loss
4.  Crop Selection:  Heat-tolerant varieties
5.  Irrigation Timing:  Evening watering during heat waves

  Drought Mitigation
1.  Drought-Resistant Varieties:  Millets, sorghum, chickpea
2.  Deficit Irrigation:  Strategic water stress at non-critical stages
3.  Moisture Conservation:  Mulch, anti-transpirants
4.  Deep Plowing:  Improves water infiltration
5.  Crop Insurance:  Financial safety net

  Flood Management
1.  Raised Beds:  Vegetable growing above flood level
2.  Drainage Channels:  Quick water removal
3.  Flood-Tolerant Varieties:  Swarna-Sub1 rice (survives 15 days submergence)
4.  Soil Amendments:  Gypsum application after floods (improves structure)

## Organic Farming Certification

  NPOP (National Programme for Organic Production)
 Conversion Period:  3 years from last chemical application
 Requirements: 
- Maintain detailed farm records
- Use only organic inputs (FYM, compost, biofertilizers)
- Annual inspections by certification body
- Soil testing annually

 Benefits: 
- Premium prices (20-30% higher)
- Access to export markets (EU, USA)
- Government subsidies available

  PGS (Participatory Guarantee System)
 For Domestic Market: 
- Lower certification costs
- Peer-review based
- Suitable for small farmers

## Farm Financial Management

  Cost of Cultivation Analysis
 Fixed Costs: 
- Land rent/ownership cost
- Equipment depreciation
- Well/tube well maintenance

 Variable Costs: 
- Seeds/seedlings
- Fertilizers & manures
- Pesticides
- Labor (sowing, weeding, harvesting)
- Irrigation (electricity/diesel)

 Break-Even Analysis: 
Revenue needed = Total Fixed + Variable Costs
Helps decide whether to continue a crop

  Income Diversification
1.  Intercropping:  Grow 2-3 crops together (maize + beans)
2.  Value Addition:  Processing (tomato sauce, potato chips)
3.  Livestock Integration:  Dairy, poultry for additional income
4.  Agro-Tourism:  Farm visits, organic food experiences
5.  Contract Farming:  Guaranteed buyers, stable income

## Post-Harvest Management

  Harvesting Best Practices
-  Timing:  Early morning (lower respiration rate)
-  Tools:  Sharp knives (reduces damage)
-  Handling:  Gently to avoid bruising
-  Containers:  Ventilated crates (not plastic bags)

  Storage Solutions
 Cold Storage: 
- Tomato: 12-15°C (7-10 days)
- Potato: 2-4°C (3-6 months)
- Onion: 0-1°C (6-8 months)

 Ambient Storage: 
- Onion: Ventilated sheds (3-4 months)
- Garlic: Low humidity (6-7 months)

  Value Addition Opportunities
-  Sun Drying:  Tomato, onion, leafy greens
-  Pickling:  Chili, lemon, vegetables
-  Canning:  Tomato puree, sauces
-  Packaging:  Washed, graded vegetables for retail

## Government Schemes for Farmers

  PMKSY (Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana)
 Subsidies: 
- Drip irrigation: 55% for general farmers, 75% for SC/ST
- Sprinklers: 45% subsidy
- Farm ponds: ₹1.5-2 lakh per hectare

  PM-KISAN (Income Support)
- ₹6,000 per year direct bank transfer
- All landholding farmers eligible
- 3 installments of ₹2,000 each

  Crop Insurance (PMFBY)
- Premium: 2% for Kharif, 1.5% for Rabi
- Covers yield loss due to natural calamities
- Sum insured = Average yield × MSP

  KCC (Kisan Credit Card)
- Short-term crop loans at 7% interest
- If repaid on time: 3% subvention → Effective 4%
- Additional 3% for timely repayment → Effective 1%

## Emergency Preparedness

  Weather Disaster Response
 Cyclone: 
- Harvest mature crops immediately
- Provide drainage channels
- Stake/support tall plants

 Heatwave: 
- Increase irrigation frequency
- Apply mulch
- Spray anti-transpirants

 Frost: 
- Smoke generation (prevents temperature drop)
- Irrigation night before (water releases heat)
- Row covers for sensitive crops

  Market Crash Response
- Immediately stop harvesting (if perishable)
- Find alternative markets (online, direct)
- Process into value-added products
- Feed to livestock if no buyers
